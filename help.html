Recorder — help

Record once. Everyone practices.

The recorder sits in a panel beside your application while you perform a task the ordinary way. It keeps a picture of every screen you pass through, and when you are finished it sends the run to Captcher, which turns it into a walkthrough other people can click through for themselves.

You do not have to narrate, script or pause. Do the task as you would on any other day. The recorder watches for the screen changing and saves each new screen on its own.

Before your first recording

The recorder has to be linked to your Captcher account before it can record anything — it needs somewhere to send your walkthroughs. This is a one-off, there is nothing to copy or type, and the one thing it depends on is that you are signed in to Captcher: the recorder gets its key from the signed-in page itself, so a signed-out page cannot give it one.

  1. Open the recorder panel

    Click the Captcher icon in the browser toolbar. The panel opens on the right, and until this browser is connected it opens straight onto Set up the recorder.

  2. Go to Captcher and sign in

    Press Open Captcher in that card — it names the address it will go to — or open Captcher in the tab yourself. Sign in the way you normally would.

  3. That is the whole of it

    The panel connects itself the moment it sees the signed-in page, with nothing pressed, and the card changes to Connected with your email address. If it says Sign in to finish connecting instead, the page is Captcher but nobody is signed in on it — sign in and press Try again.

Connected browsers are listed on your Captcher account page, so you can see what is connected and remove anything you no longer use.

Record a walkthrough

  1. Open the screen where the task begins

    Go to the page in your application that a learner would start from. The recorder captures a real product, so whatever is on screen is what people will see.

  2. Name it, and press Start recording

    Give the walkthrough a title — Approve a purchase order, Add a new patient — so you can find it later. Leave the box empty and the page title is used instead.

  3. Do the task

    Click, type and navigate exactly as you normally would. Each time the screen changes in a meaningful way, the recorder briefly takes over the page to save it. You will see a short notice while that happens — a second or two — and then you carry on.

  4. Press Finish & submit

    The run is sent to Captcher, where it becomes a draft walkthrough waiting for you. The panel confirms it and then clears itself, ready for the next one.

What the panel is telling you while you record

  • A small coral dot beside the title pulses while a recording is live.
  • Captured counts the screens saved so far.
  • Pending counts the clicks and entries recorded since the last screen was saved. It goes back to nothing each time a screen is captured.
  • Below the buttons, a picture of every screen captured so far, newest at the top. If a screen you expected is missing, that is where you will notice.

What the person using your application sees

While a screen is being saved, the page is covered briefly and clicks are ignored, so that nothing moves halfway through being photographed. Three short notices appear, in order:

Getting ready to capture The page changed. Input is paused while Captcher prepares a snapshot.
Capturing this page Captcher is saving a snapshot of this screen. Please don't click or type.
Page captured This screen has been saved to your walkthrough.

If you click during that pause, the click is neither performed nor recorded. Wait for the notice to clear and click again.

Capturing a screen yourself

Most changes are picked up on their own, but some are too quiet to notice — a form you have filled in but not yet submitted, a small message in a corner, a screen you want kept exactly as it is now. Press Capture step and the screen in front of you is saved as a step.

Starting over

Discard throws the recording away, screens and all. It asks first, because those screens cannot be recovered. To keep what you have, use Finish & submit instead — a short walkthrough is still a walkthrough, and you can edit it in Captcher afterwards.

Settings

Everything below lives behind the Settings button in the panel header.

Capture steps automatically

On by default, and where it should stay for most recordings: the recorder saves a screen whenever the page changes — a pop-up opens, a panel slides in, the screen updates. Turn it off on a busy page that updates by itself — a live dashboard, a feed, a ticking clock — and capture each step yourself with Capture step.

Appearance

Dark and Light are what they say. System follows your computer and changes with it — including on a sunrise schedule, if you use one. Light is the default. Whatever you pick applies to this help page too, and to every window where the recorder is open.

Window size

A walkthrough is replayed at the shape it was recorded at, so the size of your window is part of the recording. Pick one of the preset sizes — or type your own — before you start, and every screen in the walkthrough will match. Two numbers are shown, and they differ: Window is the whole browser window, and Page area is what is actually photographed, which is the window minus the tabs, the toolbar and this panel.

Capture a single page

One screen on its own rather than a recorded run. Useful for a reference screen or a quick test that everything is connected. Most walkthroughs do not need it.

Show log

A running account of what the recorder is doing, in its own words. You do not need it, but if you report a problem, opening it and copying the last lines is the single most useful thing you can send along.

Reset panel

Restarts the panel when it has got stuck. It drops anything you are part-way through recording, so finish or discard first if what you have is worth keeping.

If something is not working

Start recording is greyed out The browser is not connected to Captcher yet. Open Settings and follow the connection line at the top.
“The active tab can’t be recorded” The tab is not an ordinary web page — a browser settings page, the extensions page, a PDF or a new tab. Open your application in a normal tab and try again.
A screen you expected was not captured The change was too small to notice, or it happened while the previous screen was still being saved. Press Capture step to save it yourself. If it keeps happening on the same page, capture that whole flow by hand.
Too many screens are being captured The page is updating on its own. Turn off Capture steps automatically in Settings and capture the steps yourself.
The page seems frozen A capture is in progress; the notice on the page says so. It clears on its own. If it is still there after a minute, use Reset panel in Settings — it releases the page.
A capture failed, or took very long Large pages with a lot of images take longer. Try the step again. If it fails repeatedly, turn on Show log and send us the last few lines.
Sending failed Your recording is kept — nothing is lost. Check that you are still signed in to Captcher, then press Finish & submit again.
The panel has lost its connection Open Settings with Captcher open in the tab. It reconnects on its own, and tells you if it cannot.

A few things worth knowing

  • Recognized password, one-time-code and card controls (including a password revealed with a show-password button), plus hidden inputs, are blanked during capture and restored afterwards. Screenshots are omitted when these controls or embedded frames are present. Capture stops if field protection cannot be verified, including inaccessible embedded frames.
  • Redaction does not identify every secret in ordinary text, URLs, images or unmarked fields. Record with test data rather than real credentials or customer records, and review the resulting draft before sharing it.
  • The recording follows you between tabs and pages within the flow, so a task that opens a new tab still records as one walkthrough.
  • What arrives in Captcher is a draft. Nothing is published until you review it.
  • Closing the panel stops recording and loses unsent captures. Keep it open until you finish submitting.
